00

What this is, and how it teaches

A single small application, documented seam by seam. The application is a teaching device; the method is the point — borrowed, deliberately, from the most widely-read book on software boundaries.

The running example. A support-ticket triage console. An agent reads an incoming ticket stream, drafts replies, sets priority, and can request escalation; a rare human operator — the host — can take the same actions through the same commands; and the customer receives a resolution without ever touching the interface. It is the same illustrative scenario the reference uses. Nothing in the architecture depends on it — substitute your own domain freely.

The method, borrowed from Clean Architecture. That book does not catalogue boundaries abstractly; it traces a single request across each one in turn, names a concrete participant at every hop, and hands a simple data structure across each seam. We do the same. First the picture and the one law (below); then, for readers who already know Clean Architecture, the exact mapping of its vocabulary onto ours (§ mapping); then a single command traced end to end (§ a typical scenario); then the whole program assembled (§ the whole example); then the seam pages, each running the same eight-slot template (§ how to read a seam); and last, which rung of the reference's adoption ladder each of those pages actually walks, and where this walkthrough stops (§ which rung each seam walks).

The one move worth holding in mind

A human action and an agent action are the same signed Command on one append-only, replayable stream, differing only by the Signature the boundary stamps on it — who acted, and under whose permission. Every seam below exists to keep that true. When a seam's purpose is unclear, ask: what would stop being replayable, or pure, or overridable, if this seam were removed? That question — “what breaks without it” — is the eighth slot of every seam page.

01

The rings, and the one law

Clean Architecture draws concentric rings and asserts a single rule over them: source-code dependencies may only point inward, toward higher-level, more stable policy. This architecture keeps that rule and sharpens it.

The law, in one sentence. An import may point inward toward the core, or it is the composition root; it may never point outward from the core, sideways between adapters, or from a passive node — a surface or a tool — into anything but domain types. That is the canonical wording, used unchanged wherever this rule is declared. Its consequence here is the sharper claim this example is built to show: purity points inward, and only the boundary adapter is impure. Concretely: the State and the fold import nothing foreign; tools, the projection, and the port interfaces are pure and depend only on the core; and every impure act — minting identity, reading the clock, stamping the signature, resolving an authority, gating an irreversible action, committing to the bus, keying and performing an effect — is confined to a single seam. Clean Architecture scatters impurity across its entire outer ring (controllers, presenters, gateways, the database, the web). This architecture collapses all of it into one named place. That is the sharper, more teachable invariant, and it is the spine of everything that follows.

Dependency direction vs. flow of control

The two run opposite ways, and the difference is the whole trick. At runtime, control flows outward — the fold hands effect descriptors out to the boundary, which performs them on the world. But every source dependency points inward — the boundary names the fold; the fold names nothing outer. The device that lets control go one way while the compile-time arrow goes the other is a polymorphic interface declared on the inner side: our ports and our effect descriptors. Hold this; it recurs at every seam.

03

A typical scenario

One event, traced across every seam in sequence, a named data structure handed across each crossing. This is the spine the seam pages then deepen, one crossing at a time.

The event: a new ticket, #4118, arrives in the queue. The agent reads it and sets its priority to High. Follow the thread:

  1. Sensing (EventSource port). The TicketQueue adapter receives a raw event and normalizes it to a typed domain event, TicketArrived(4118, body), which the boundary stages for this one turn. Raw event in; no domain logic here.
  2. The clock, then the context. The boundary reads the clock once — the only read in the system — and computes projectContext(state, staged, bounds): a bounded, pure projection of committed truth plus the staged event, under the window the composition root wired. That Context is what the reasoner sees, and its rendered digest is committed at step 6. pure
  3. The verb crossing. The agent emits an Action("setPriority", {ticket:4118, level:High}) — an open name with a raw input. resolveAction looks the name up in the registry, decodes the input, and calls the verb's run(input, ctx), which returns the payload SetPriority(4118, High) and mutates nothing. An unknown name resolves to Unhandled here rather than falling through. pure
  4. The stamp & the gate (boundary adapter). The boundary mints Signature(by = Agent, authority = agent-run-7f) and runs every result past the irreversibility gate. setPriority is reversible, so it passes untouched; a confirmEscalation whose authority equals the requesting one would become Refused right here, before the fold. The signature is minted here and nowhere else.
  5. The fold. fold(state, [SetPriority(4118,High)], now, sig) checks that 4118 exists, then returns (state', [Effect.LogDecision(...)]) — a new immutable state and an effect descriptor. It performs nothing. pure
  6. The commit. append(bus, StepRecord{schemaVersion, now, sig, staged, actions, results, commands, context}) writes the whole step to the append-only log and returns its index. Nothing happens off the record — and now rides the record, or a re-fold could not reproduce it.
  7. The effect, performed (boundary adapter). perform(KeyedEffect(EffectKey(index, 0), Effect.LogDecision(...)), LIVE). The one impure act in the whole trace — and its key could not exist until step 6 returned the index.
  8. The projection & surface. project(state') recomputes the view: ticket 4118's row now reads badge:"HIGH", canEscalate:true. The surface renders it. It decides nothing — it applies flags already decided. pure

Notice the asymmetry made concrete: at steps 3–7 control flows outward (verb → boundary → world), while every source dependency along the way points inward at the fold and the State. That is the law of §01, walked.

The whole example, assembled

Every fragment the seam pages dissect, in one place, in neutral pseudocode. This is the canonical source: the names here are the names everywhere. Copy it, then read the seams to learn why each line sits where it does.

support-ticket triage console — the whole programpseudocode
// ───────── Entities · the inner ring · pure, no I/O (seam 01) ─────────
enum Priority { Low, Normal, High, Urgent }
enum Actor    { Human, Agent, Spine }           // WHO acted — grows only at architecture revision
value Authority(id: Text)                       // UNDER WHOSE permission — an opaque principal, not a variant set
sealed TicketStatus(ticket: TicketId) {
  Open | Escalating(requestedBy: Authority) | Escalated(confirmedBy: Authority) | Resolved
}
type  Ticket = { id: TicketId, body: Text, priority: Priority, status: TicketStatus }
sealed Notice(at: Timestamp, tool: ToolName, reason: Text) { Rejected | Refused }  // PER-ITEM
sealed RunStatus { Idle | Working(step) | Degraded(cause) | Error(fault) }         // SESSION-GLOBAL, boundary-only
type  State = { tickets: Map<TicketId, Ticket>,   // triage + escalation
                focused: TicketId?,               // console — a folded presentation decision
                panels:  Map<PanelId, Bool>,      // console
                run:     RunStatus,               // spine — the boundary writes this, no arm does
                notices: [Notice] }               // spine — one entry per rejection or refusal

// ───────── The stamp, and the signed action that crosses the bus (seam 02) ─────────
value Signature(by: Actor, authority: Authority)             // minted at the boundary, and nowhere else — one
                                                             // production site in the app: a stamp a tool or a fold
                                                             // conjures for itself is a forged stamp (book 5.3)
sealed Command(tool: ToolName, sig: Signature, id: CmdId) {  // the parent declares all three
  SetPriority(ticket: TicketId, level: Priority)
  RequestEscalation(ticket: TicketId)                 // a request — reversible
  ConfirmEscalation(ticket: TicketId)                 // irreversible — gated at the boundary, on AUTHORITY
  FocusTicket(ticket: TicketId)                       // presentation — an authored act, so it signs
  SetPanel(panel: PanelId, visible: Bool)             // presentation — likewise
  Unhandled(note: Text) | Refused(reason: Text)       // a refusal is a decision; it signs too
}

// ───────── Tool results · the sealed payloads a verb returns (seam 03) ─────────
// The parent declares `tool`. That name is the discriminant here, the case name in
// Command, and the registry key — one name per verb. No variant may hold an Actor, an
// Authority or a Signature: the stamp does not exist yet when a verb runs.
sealed ToolResult(tool: ToolName) {
  SetPriority(ticket: TicketId, level: Priority)
  RequestEscalation(ticket: TicketId)
  ConfirmEscalation(ticket: TicketId)
  FocusTicket(ticket: TicketId)
  SetPanel(panel: PanelId, visible: Bool)
  Unhandled(note: Text)              // no registered verb, or an input that failed to decode
  Refused(reason: Text)              // the boundary gate said no — committed, so it re-folds
}

// ───────── Verbs · ONE mechanic, one registration, two maps (seam 03) ─────────
// Registering a verb forces the reversibility decision — there is no default to fall into.
sealed Verb(name: ToolName, describe: Text, decode: RawInput -> Input?,
            run: (Input, Ctx) -> ToolResult, sign: (ToolResult, Signature, CmdId) -> Command) {
  Reversible
  Irreversible(requestedBy: (State, ToolResult) -> Authority?)   // says where its matching Request lives
}
data Ctx = { state: State, context: Context }   // what a verb may read. No actor. No authority.

// A DOMAIN VERB — pure. Reads read-only ctx, returns a payload, mutates nothing.
VERB setPriority = Reversible {
  input = { ticket: TicketId, level: Priority }
  run(input, ctx) -> SetPriority(input.ticket, input.level)
}
// A PRESENTATION VERB — the SAME mechanic. Also pure, also folded, also signed.
VERB focusTicket = Reversible {
  input = { ticket: TicketId }
  run(input, ctx) -> FocusTicket(input.ticket)
}
// A GATED VERB — Irreversible cannot be constructed without saying where its Request lives.
VERB confirmEscalation = Irreversible {
  input = { ticket: TicketId }
  run(input, ctx) -> ConfirmEscalation(input.ticket)
  requestedBy(s, r) = (s.statusOf(r.ticket) as? Escalating)?.requestedBy
}
// … and two more of the same shape: requestEscalation (Reversible) and setPanel (Reversible).

// ───────── The context projection · the third pure map (seam 03) ─────────
data StagedInput = { source: Text, body: Text }        // untrusted perceived content — one per turn
data Context     = { staged: [StagedInput], lines: [Text], notices: [Text] }   // ORDERED off-bus fixture (book 6.11)
MAX_CONTEXT_LINES = 8 ; MAX_CONTEXT_NOTICES = 8        // the SHIPPED DEFAULT window, not the law
data ContextBounds = { lines: Int, notices: Int }       // the window, as ONE value the root wires
SHIPPED = ContextBounds(MAX_CONTEXT_LINES, MAX_CONTEXT_NOTICES)

projectContext(state, staged, bounds = SHIPPED) -> Context:   // PURE. Recomputed each step.
  Context { staged:  staged,
            lines:   ticketLines(state).takeLast(bounds.lines),
            notices: state.notices.takeLast(bounds.notices).map(summarize) }
render(context) -> Text                                // the exact text the reasoner sees — and a fixture
data ContextFixture = { promptVersion: Text, digest: Text }   // committed per step, re-checked on replay

// ───────── The fold · the Use-Case Interactor · pure (seam 01) ─────────
// Returns (newState, effects). It RETURNS effect descriptors; it never performs them.
// Total over a SEALED ToolResult, so there is NO else arm. Every arm reads current state
// first, and every effect push lives inside the success branch.
fold(state, results, now, sig) -> (State, [Effect]):
  for r in results:
    match r:
      SetPriority(tool, ticket, level) ->
        state.tickets.has(ticket)
          ? ( state.withPriority(ticket, level),
              [ Effect.LogDecision(at = now, ticket, level, supersedes = state.priorityOf(ticket)) ] )
          : ( state.withNotice(Notice.Rejected(now, tool, "unknown ticket")), [] )  // refused: no change, NO effect
      RequestEscalation(tool, ticket) ->
        state.statusOf(ticket) is Open
          ? ( state.withStatus(ticket, Escalating(requestedBy = sig.authority)),
              [] )                              // a request commits no irreversible effect
          : ( state.withNotice(Notice.Rejected(now, tool, "not open")), [] )
      ConfirmEscalation(tool, ticket) ->        // the gate already ran, at the boundary — step (5)
        state.statusOf(ticket) is Escalating
          ? ( state.withStatus(ticket, Escalated(confirmedBy = sig.authority)),
              [ Effect.PageOncall(at = now, ticket) ] )
          : ( state.withNotice(Notice.Rejected(now, tool, "no pending request")), [] )
      FocusTicket(tool, ticket) ->              // a PRESENTATION verb — folded like any other
        state.tickets.has(ticket)
          ? ( state.withFocus(ticket), [] )
          : ( state.withNotice(Notice.Rejected(now, tool, "unknown ticket")), [] )
      SetPanel(tool, panel, visible) ->
        ( state.withPanel(panel, visible), [] )
      Refused(tool, reason) ->                  // the gate's verdict, committed — so it re-folds
        ( state.withNotice(Notice.Refused(now, tool, reason)), [ Effect.Diag(at = now, reason) ] )
      Unhandled(tool, note) ->
        ( state.withNotice(Notice.Rejected(now, tool, note)), [ Effect.Diag(at = now, note) ] )

// Effect descriptors — plain data, and NO identity of their own. The parent declares `at`.
// Performed only at the boundary (seam 01 → 02), and only wrapped in a KeyedEffect.
sealed Effect(at: Timestamp) { LogDecision(...) | PageOncall(...) | Diag(note) }

// ───────── What the boundary is handed, and what it commits (seam 02) ─────────
data Action       = { tool: ToolName, input: RawInput }   // an OPEN name — the one open input in the system
data FinishedStep = { by: Actor, staged: [StagedInput], actions: [Action] }
data StepRecord   = { schemaVersion: SchemaVersion,   // 14.7 — which shape the rest is in
                      now: Timestamp, sig: Signature, staged: [StagedInput],
                      actions:  [Action],        // what was ASKED
                      results:  [ToolResult],    // what was FOLDED — post-gate; the two differ when refused
                      commands: [Command], context: ContextFixture }
data EffectKey    = { step: StepIndex, index: Int }    // from the COMMITTED sequence — the fold cannot mint it
data KeyedEffect  = { key: EffectKey, effect: Effect } // the only thing `perform` accepts

// The boundary's two closed maps, both fed by the ONE registration a verb gets.
resolveAction(registry, action, ctx) -> ToolResult =        // name → ToolResult, run BEFORE the fold
  registry[action.tool]
    ? registry[action.tool].decode(action.input)
        ? registry[action.tool].run(decoded, ctx)
        : Unhandled(action.tool, "input failed to decode")
    : Unhandled(action.tool, "no registered verb")

gate(r, sig, state, registry, authz) -> ToolResult =        // the irreversibility gate — keyed on AUTHORITY
  match registry[r.tool]:
    Reversible          -> r
    Irreversible(reqBy) -> match reqBy(state, r):
        null            -> Refused(r.tool, "no pending request")
        sig.authority   -> Refused(r.tool, "self-confirm: the confirming authority is the requesting one")
        _               -> authz.mayConfirm(sig, r, state) ? r : Refused(r.tool, "authority may not confirm")

// ───────── The boundary adapter · the ONE impure seam (seam 02) ─────────
boundary.onStepFinish(step: FinishedStep):
  now     = clock.now()                                    // (1) the ONLY clock read in the system (G9)
  ctx     = Ctx(state, projectContext(state, step.staged, bounds)) // (2) the third pure projection
  results = step.actions.map(a -> resolveAction(registry, a, ctx))   // (3) the ONE name → ToolResult map
  sig     = Signature(by = step.by,                        // (4) the stamp — WHO, and under whose permission
                      authority = authz.authorityOf(step.by, session))
  gated   = results.map(r -> gate(r, sig, state, registry, authz))   // (5) PRE-FOLD, so the verdict commits
  (s, fx) = fold(state, gated, now, sig)                   // (6) pure call — decide
  index   = append(bus, StepRecord {                       // (7) COMMIT the step, not the pair
              schemaVersion, now, sig,                     //     the envelope rides the record (14.7)
              staged: step.staged, actions: step.actions, results: gated,
              commands: gated.map(r -> sign(registry, r, sig, nextId())),   // EVERY verb signs
              context:  ContextFixture(promptVersion, render(ctx.context)) })
  state   = s                                              // (8) adopt the new truth
  for (i, f) in fx:                                        // (9) the only place effects run —
    perform(KeyedEffect(EffectKey(index, i), f), mode = LIVE)   //     keyed from the COMMITTED index

// ───────── The view projection · the Presenter · pure (seam 04) ─────────
project(state) -> ConsoleView:
  ConsoleView {
    rows: state.tickets.values.map(t -> TicketRow {
      ticket:      t.id,
      badge:       badgeFor(t.priority),        // "HIGH" + colour, pre-computed
      focused:     t.id == state.focused,       // the folded presentation decision, read back out
      canEscalate: match t.status {             // a CLOSED match, not `t.status == Open` —
        Open -> true | Escalating -> false      //   a fifth status must break the build here
        Escalated -> false | Resolved -> false },
      escalating:  t.status is Escalating }),
    panels:  state.panels,
    notices: state.notices.map(summarize),      // per-item failures surface as notices …
    banner:  bannerFor(state.run)               // … never as the session banner
  }

// ───────── The surface · the View · humble (seam 04) ─────────
render(view: ConsoleView):
  for row in view.rows: drawRow(row)            // no `if` over data, no computed flag
onAction(a: Action) ->                          // one sink — the SAME entry the agent loop uses
  boundary.onStepFinish(FinishedStep(by = Human, staged = null, actions = [a]))

// ───────── The ports · interfaces the core owns (seam 05) ─────────
PORT Clock         { now() -> Timestamp }                              // read once per step, at (1)
PORT IdSource      { next() -> CmdId }                                 // identity, minted at (7)
PORT Bus           { append(StepRecord) -> StepIndex; records() -> [StepRecord] }
PORT Sink          { perform(KeyedEffect, mode: LIVE|REPLAY|RECOVERY) }
PORT Authorization { authorityOf(Actor, session) -> Authority          // the product-owned seam
                     mayConfirm(Signature, ToolResult, State) -> Bool }
PORT Runtime       { onAction(Action); observe() -> Stream<ConsoleView> }  // the domain port
PORT ModelProvider { step(prompt, tools) -> StepResult }               // the model backend
PORT EventSource   { events() -> Stream<RawEvent> }                     // sensing (tickets in)

// ───────── The composition root · the only wiring site (seam 06) ─────────
wireApp(env):
  bus      = Bus()
  clock    = env.live ? SystemClock() : FixedClock(env.t0)
  authz    = env.live ? HostAuthority(env.session) : ScriptedAuthority(env.fixtures)
  model    = env.live ? HttpModel(env.url) : ScriptedModel(env.fixtures)   // ← the swap
  sink     = env.live ? LiveSinks(pager, store) : RecordingSink()
  sensing  = TicketQueue(env.queue)
  registry = register(triage) + register(escalation) + register(console)   // one line per block — the ports register THREE MORE (artifact, analysis, inbox), elided here
  boundary = Boundary(clock, ids, bus, sink, authz, registry, fold, projectContext)
  return App(boundary, sensing, model)

// ───────── The replay harness · tests are the outer ring (seam 07) ─────────
replayTest(live):                               // live = a real run: its bus, its state, its performed effects
  (state, effects) = refold(initialState, live.bus.records())   // re-fold ONLY the committed bytes
  assert state   == live.state                  // the same truth …
  assert effects == live.performed              // … and the same effect sequence: keys AND every `at`
  collectPerform(live.bus.records(), replaySink, mode = REPLAY)
  assert replaySink.performed == live.performed // descriptors collected …
  assert world.untouched()                      // … and NOTHING fired
Four things the listing makes structural, not conventional

One name per verb. setPriority is the registry key, the ToolResult case and the Command case. One registration supplies both maps — run (name → ToolResult) and sign (name → Command) — so a recorded result can never disagree with what was folded.

The stamp is unrepresentable upstream. No ToolResult case and no Ctx field has a type that can hold an Actor. A verb cannot claim who is asking, because at line (3) the answer has not been minted yet; it appears at line (4). The gate then keys on the Authority, never the Actor.

The fold cannot key an effect. Effect has no identity field, so there is nothing for a fold arm to mint into. The key is EffectKey(committed index, position) and it is unavailable until line (7) has returned — which is what makes “commit before perform” unwritable-wrong rather than merely conventional.

There is one tool mechanic. focusTicket and setPanel are verbs with the same shape, the same signer, the same commit path and the same cost to add as setPriority. An agent that rearranges the interface is doing an authored, auditable act; there is no cheaper UI path, because there is no UI path.

Read it once for shape, not detail. The seam pages take it apart one crossing at a time — and every snippet they show is lifted verbatim from here, so the program always chains.

05

How to read a seam

Every seam page — all seven — runs the same eight-slot template. Learn the slots once; then each page is a five-minute read and the uniformity does the teaching.

The seam
Its name, and a one-line statement of the conversation it carries.
Each side
The responsibility on the inner side and on the outer side — policy vs. mechanism, in one sentence each.
What crosses
The exact data structure that may cross — and, loudly, the forbidden payload that may not (an entity, a live handle, the clock).
Direction
Which way the source dependency points (inward) and which way control flows (often outward).
The inversion
The polymorphic interface, declared on the inner side, that lets control oppose the dependency.
Why it exists
The volatility it quarantines, or the property (replay, purity, override) it protects.
What breaks
The concrete failure if you violate it — shown as a collapsed anti-example, with the test that dies.
The swap / the test
What becomes testable once the seam exists — usually: the whole session replays off-target.

Start at seam 01 — State & the fold and walk through to 07. The pages assume you have read this overview; they do not re-explain the law or the example, they apply them. One map is worth carrying in with you, and it is the last thing on this page: which rung of the reference's adoption ladder each of those seven pages walks.

06

Which rung each seam walks

The reference sorts the architecture into an adoption ladder — an irreducible core, then four rungs you climb only when a named failure in your own app asks for one — and it deliberately says nothing there about who has climbed what. Here is that ladder met from this example's side: for each rung, the page that walks it, and what the walkthrough actually puts in front of you. A reading map, not a scoreboard.

Where this walkthrough stops — said here, not discovered later

These pages are pseudocode, and that is the whole of what they claim. § the whole example says so on its own banner: the listing fixes the names and the shape, so one vocabulary carries across every seam page and up into the reference. None of these listings compiles, and nothing on this page is executed by any build. That is true of the walkthrough, not of everything it points at: where one of these pages quotes a measured fact about an accompanying implementation — a count, a timing — that implementation's own build is what keeps the number honest, and a walkthrough is the wrong place to look for the receipt. A rung drawn well in a walkthrough is not evidence that it works — evidence is a property of running code, and it belongs beside the code it is a property of, stated there and measured by a build that either goes green or does not.

Two rungs are drawn at lower resolution than the rest. The mailbox and the relay live in seam 07's advanced section, four lines each, and never get an eight-slot page of their own. That is deliberate — they are opt-in and the seven core seams are not — but it does mean the walkthrough teaches them thinner than it teaches the core, and you should read the reference's own chapters before taking either rung.

And one guarantee this example cannot hand you at all. Single-writer confinement of a turn is a property of the shape — the consumer mints the turn's one channel and calls the boundary itself — and no build check anywhere can enforce it. Keep the shape and you keep the guarantee; run a turn on another thread and fold from there, and two folds interleave with every other rule on these pages still satisfied.
